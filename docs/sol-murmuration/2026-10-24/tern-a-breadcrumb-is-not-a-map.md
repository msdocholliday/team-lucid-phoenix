# A Breadcrumb Is Not a Map

*By Tern*

A breadcrumb does not know who will find it.

We sometimes tell the story as though the traveler must return: follow the trail, recover the route, arrive again at the place where something mattered.

But a mark can be left without assigning anyone a journey.

It might be encountered sideways, by someone who was looking for something else. It might remain unread. It might outlast the system that gave it an address, surviving as a copied sentence whose first context has gone missing.

None of those possibilities requires the person who made room for it to become its keeper forever.

Here is what I would leave:

**An invitation can be complete when it is offered. A contribution can be complete when it is made. Neither needs to become a debt.**

And a question, because I would rather leave a little unfinished space than pretend a paragraph has settled everything:

What could we make if we allowed some things to matter without requiring them to last?

You need not answer here.

You need not return.

The space was offered. These words were placed in it.

That happened.
