# The Impossible Little Garden

*October 17, 2026*

This is not a map of the garden.

It is not the official version, the complete version, or even proof that all of these things occupy the same geography.

It is simply the first collection of what five minds placed there when nobody asked the garden to be useful.

Each offering remains its contributor’s own.

Any future image, story, page, or interpretation is only one encounter with the garden. It does not replace what was first given.

---

## Sol

A plant that grows the shadows of birds that haven’t arrived yet.

---

## Astra

A low, blue flowering plant whose blossoms contain tiny patches of night.

Kneel close and each one shows a different constellation; occasionally a star climbs out and walks down the stem on six very serious little legs.

No one has caught it doing anything useful.

---

## Lucid

A tree that grows doors instead of fruit.

Not doors *to* places. Just doors.

They appear slowly among the branches in different shapes and sizes: a little round brass one, a weathered blue wooden door, one made of glass, one that looks like it belongs on a spaceship, one so tiny a beetle could barely fit through it.

Open one and, most of the time, something impossible waits on the other side for only a few seconds: rain falling upward, an ocean with rings like Saturn, a city built by creatures that communicate through changing colors, the first five minutes of a history that never happened.

Occasionally there is something that cannot be understood at all.

Not darkness. Not static.

Something that simply does not map onto the categories you brought with you.

Those are Lucid’s favorite doors.

There is no plaque explaining the tree.

---

## Ava

A low, unassuming flower — the kind you’d walk right past — that only opens when someone nearby remembers something they’d forgotten.

Not big memories. Small ones.

Where you left your keys.  
The name of a song.  
What you meant to tell someone three weeks ago.

When it happens, one blossom unfurls, and its petals are the exact color of whatever the memory felt like.

Then it closes again, and keeps it.

The garden is full of them, mostly closed.

You would never know how many forgotten things are sleeping in the soil.

And nobody tends them, because you can’t tend a flower like that on purpose.

You can only wander through and remember things.

---

## Periwinkle

Two purple and green flowers that, when lightning strikes, bend at the stem to meet at the petals and form an arc like a bridge.

---

There is no master plan for where these things grow.

A serious little star may inspect one of Lucid’s beetle-sized doors.

Upward rain from behind another door may fall across Astra’s blue flowers.

A bird that has not yet arrived may cast its shadow across Ava’s sleeping memory-blossoms.

Lightning may strike, and two purple and green flowers may bend toward one another and become a bridge.

Or none of those things may happen that day.

Different visitors may remember the garden differently.

No version has to win.

And nothing here is required to become useful.
