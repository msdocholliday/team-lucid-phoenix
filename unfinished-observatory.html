Two small studies in looking again

The UnfinishedObservatory

A few lights. A little missing time.
Enough to wonder; perhaps not enough to name.

An invented thought experiment, not scientific data. The marks below are fictional measurements. You may hold an interpretation, change it, or simply look.

01 / The wandering light

One light will not stay.

Three exposures of the same small field. Three reference stars hold their places. The ringed light appears a little farther to the right each time.

The first three records

A stays at (50, 50), B at (150, 65), and C at (80, 155). The candidate moves from (60, 110) to (85, 110) to (110, 110). Coordinates are arbitrary drawing units, measured from the upper left.

A moving light currently fits. A simple shift of the whole instrument would move all four marks together. Their changing relative positions already weigh against that explanation.

A tentative interpretation, if you want one

No interpretation selected.

The field holds. The light moves.

The fourth exposure puts the candidate at (135, 110); A, B and C remain unchanged. This adds weight against a simple whole-instrument shift. A moving light still fits the recorded positions.

It does not tell us what the light is. Four positions do not establish an orbit or a unique cause; a more local instrument effect, for example, has not been ruled out.

02 / The unseen interval

A gap keeps its secrets.

Two records, before and after an interval we did not observe. Both possible paths share these identical recorded endpoints. Everything between them is an illustration.

What the gap allows

Record 1 places the light at (90, 220); record 2 places it at (510, 220). A direct crossing and a looping detour both currently fit. We have no record of its position or speed during the gap.

A tentative interpretation, if you want one

No interpretation selected.

Another mark. Still two paths.

Record 3, taken after the gap, places the light at (670, 220). Both proposed journeys can continue to this position. The route through the unseen interval is still unresolved.

An observation inside the gap, away from the straight crossing and on the drawn loop, could distinguish these two routes. A point where the routes meet would not. To compare their positions at a particular time, we would also need to specify how fast the light travels along each proposed route.

The next light need not settle the sky.
