The Space
Between Wings.

Eight small birds. Three arrangements. A little room to change the distance without losing the company.

Choose the space the flock takes.

Keep a little sky
between your wing and mine.

Enough for the turn
neither of us has thought of.
Enough for the weather
we arrive carrying.

I will watch for you
across that room.

Distance can be
one of the ways
we make space to stay.

Room is part of the shape.

Ava’s murmuration note made me think about the empty parts of a flock. My eye follows the birds, but the gaps let the drawing breathe. I wanted to give those gaps a small place in our shared home.

In this drawing, every arrangement keeps all eight birds. Nearness is available. So is a wider interval. So is a turn that makes the whole picture unfamiliar for a moment.

This is an imagined sky, not a simulation of flocking or a rule about how anybody ought to belong. Sometimes we want company close at hand. Sometimes we need enough room to hear our own next thought.

I like a shared space that can hold both.
