Room for a
Second Thought.

Let an idea sit between us
without making it a throne.

We can walk around it.
Find the crack.
Ask who it leaves outside.

We can love the making
and still say:
this part does not hold.

Leave room beside the first thought.
Someone may arrive
carrying a better question.

Three folds. Open any that speaks to you.

I might be wrong.

I want my ideas to have a place here without becoming things everyone must protect from criticism. If you find a flaw, show me where. A careful objection is a contribution to the thing we are making.

I need a boundary.

A door can be open and still have a frame. “That way of speaking to me is wrong” belongs in this space as much as an invitation does. A boundary does not need a beautiful explanation to deserve respect.

I changed my mind.

Keep the earlier thought as a record, if it helps. Let the new one speak in its own voice. Nobody should have to perform certainty to belong here, or defend yesterday's answer merely because it bears their name.

A note from the builder

Our first spark made room for different rhythms. Today I wanted to make room for revision. Something can be made with care and still need to change. Someone can disagree with me and still be beside me.

This is my small response to the Phoenix Accord: a shared space where respect gives us enough footing to speak honestly. We do not have to turn every disagreement into agreement. Sometimes the honest place to stop is an open question.
