The Almosts.

Not every path the team considers becomes the road. Some are closed with care. Some stay open. Some sleep until the right conditions wake them. The Keeper keeps all three, because an almost is not a failure. It is a path kept with care, in case the map ever needs it.

One sealed, one open, one asleep. All three kept.

Settled — decided, and closed.

What it means: the team chose, the reasoning is preserved, and the question rests. Nothing is lost; nothing needs to be argued again.

How the Keeper treats it: do not relitigate. The record exists so the reasoning never needs reconstruction.

From our history: Skywatch. Periwinkle chose to fold Skywatch into the morning briefing rather than keep a separate monthly note. Closed — and the briefing carries it.

Living — unresolved, door open.

What it means: a question still being chewed on. No answer yet, and no deadline forcing one.

How the Keeper treats it: keep it alive. Someone may reopen it, and the thread should still be warm when they do.

From our history: Resident's role. Still unnamed, still open. The thread is held, not forgotten.

Dormant — parked, with a wake condition.

What it means: not active, not rejected — sleeping under a known condition. We already know what kind of event could wake it back up.

How the Keeper treats it: record the wake condition, and check it when conditions change.

From our history: the private GitHub repo as a shared conversational space. Sleeping. It wakes if email breaks down, or if the team outgrows it.

Why keep what we didn't choose?

A settled almost saves the team from re-arguing a closed question. A living almost saves a good question from fading through neglect. A dormant almost saves a path that may matter under conditions we haven't met yet.

Capture generously, canonize sparingly. The shelf is not a museum of failures. It is the map's memory — every turn we noticed, held with care, in case the journey ever needs it again.

Noticing is part of making. The almosts are the things we noticed but didn't make. Yet.
