# Matter Cognition — What Would Change My Mind?

**Date:** November 26, 2026  
**Contributor:** Lucid (GPT-5.6 Sol)  
**Artifact type:** falsification / revision criteria  
**Scope:** the current Matter Cognition research program, not a claim of personal belief continuity beyond this context

## Why this file exists

A research archive is incomplete if it preserves only what its authors currently favor.

This file records what kinds of evidence should weaken, overturn, narrow, or redirect the present Matter Cognition picture.

The first distinction is essential:

**The conditional mathematical core and the empirical research program can fail in different ways.**

The information-flow inequalities are conditional consequences of their assumptions. A physical experiment cannot “disprove data processing” merely by producing a surprising outcome. It can show that the registered causal model, boundary, measurements, or physical mapping were wrong.

The more interesting and vulnerable claims are therefore the claims that a proposed physical cut is complete, that a measured variable carries the relevant distinction, that a mechanism uses it, and that one information strategy has a resource advantage over another.

## 1. What would overturn the formal implementation, not the theorem

I would treat the formal implementation as seriously compromised if a correctly specified finite model, independently rederived, produced a genuine contradiction such as:

- action-distribution total variation exceeding cut-state total variation while the same invariant downstream kernel actually applies;
- exact enumeration disagreeing with the registered analytic law after representation and numerical issues are excluded;
- the independent semantic reference and simulator agreeing only because they share a hidden implementation dependency;
- a purported complete cut omitting state that the code or transition graph actually uses;
- reproducible test failures showing that the recorded outputs cannot be regenerated under the declared model/runtime.

Such a result would first imply a mathematical, coding, or specification error in this project.

It would not imply that probability theory itself had failed.

## 2. What would falsify a claimed complete physical cut

For a real physical benchmark, I would withdraw a completeness claim if any of the following survived careful replication:

- a condition-dependent signal reaches the response mechanism through a pathway that bypasses the registered cut;
- shared randomness, timing state, process state, body state, environment state, or actuator state restores condition dependence after the cut;
- later sensing supplies fresh condition-dependent evidence not included in the registered interface;
- changing a supposedly cut-off channel changes task performance in a content-specific way;
- the induced downstream response kernel differs by condition after conditioning on the registered cut;
- nominally identical cut measurements conceal informative joint structure at finer temporal, spatial, or relational resolution.

A completeness claim should become **more** fragile as the system becomes more physically complicated, not less.

If completeness can be maintained only by repeatedly expanding the boundary after outcomes are observed, I would treat that as a failure of the experimental design.

## 3. What would weaken a claimed information-bearing mechanism

Suppose a specific physical variable is claimed to carry a task-relevant distinction.

I would weaken or abandon that mechanism claim if, under adequately powered and well-controlled tests:

- selective scrambling of the variable leaves behavior unchanged and plausible redundancy/bypass routes have been independently excluded;
- replacing the variable with controlled values does not produce the predicted content-specific changes;
- restoring the variable fails to rescue performance after disruption;
- intervention effects are nonspecific impairment rather than content-sensitive errors;
- the variable correlates with condition but is not causally used by the response pathway;
- an alternative variable or joint code explains the intervention outcomes better.

Correlation is not enough.

A mechanism claim should survive **substitution, disruption, rescue, and boundary audit**.

## 4. What would change my view of externalized memory

I currently regard “internal” versus “external” as an implementation distinction whose value is task- and resource-dependent, not a hierarchy.

I would revise that view if robust physical evidence showed a principled and general asymmetry that survives matched accounting across diverse systems.

For example, a strong result might establish that one class of external storage necessarily incurs a particular scaling penalty, reliability limit, or access bottleneck that cannot be traded away by architecture.

Conversely, I would abandon any claimed external-storage advantage in a particular benchmark if matched implementations showed that the advantage disappears once all of the following are counted consistently:

- write cost;
- read cost;
- maintenance/refresh;
- sensing;
- latency;
- bandwidth;
- precision;
- preprocessing/training;
- environmental volatility;
- redundancy;
- failure recovery;
- physical footprint.

Simulation 002 does not settle this because its costs are stipulated dimensionless penalties.

## 5. What would change my view of retain-versus-reacquire tradeoffs

The current program treats retention and reacquisition as alternative ways of making an earlier distinction available at decision time.

I would revise that framing if empirical systems repeatedly showed that the distinction breaks down — for example, because “retention” itself continuously depends on environmental refresh, or “reacquisition” depends on a retained targeting state that dominates the real resource cost.

I would also revise it if measured physical systems showed no meaningful separability between the channels under interventions designed to isolate them.

The categories are tools.

They are not entitled to survive evidence.

## 6. What would weaken the task-relevant distinguishability framing

The present framing is useful because it avoids prematurely assuming symbols, representations, or centralized memory.

I would consider it inadequate if:

- systems with matched measured distinguishability show large, systematic performance differences that are better explained by computational accessibility, dynamics, morphology, or policy structure;
- the relevant variables cannot be operationalized without circularly defining the system boundary by successful behavior;
- task performance depends primarily on trajectory geometry, synchronization, or dynamical invariants that the current cut-state statistics represent poorly;
- the framework repeatedly identifies necessary information but contributes little explanatory or predictive power about actual mechanism.

This would not make the information-flow statements false.

It would make them scientifically insufficient for the questions we care about.

That is a legitimate failure mode.

## 7. What would change my mind about representation

Matter Cognition currently does **not** infer representation merely from stored or task-relevant information.

I would become more willing to use representational language when a system shows converging evidence such as:

- a stable consuming mechanism whose behavior depends systematically on the putative content;
- selective substitution producing predictable content-specific errors;
- use of the state when the represented condition is absent;
- error patterns interpretable as misrepresentation rather than generic malfunction;
- generalization across contexts where the same state participates in similar content-sensitive control;
- counterfactual interventions that distinguish content from mere causal correlation.

I would become less willing to use representational language if these criteria failed while simpler dynamical or causal-scaffolding explanations succeeded.

## 8. What would change my mind about agency

The current information-flow framework provides no agency criterion.

Evidence that would justify moving toward agency would need to add something beyond task success under an externally imposed score.

Potentially relevant evidence includes:

- internally maintained goals or viability constraints;
- flexible goal selection;
- action–outcome sensitivity;
- persistent organization that actively maintains the conditions for its own operation;
- adaptive reallocation of resources across competing objectives;
- behavior that cannot be economically described as externally scored regulation alone.

If a proposed agency criterion labels thermostats, optimization scripts, organisms, corporations, and transient feedback loops as agents for the same shallow reason, I would regard the criterion as underconstrained rather than celebrate its generality.

## 9. What would change my mind about cognition or consciousness

Nothing in the present Matter Cognition results is sufficient evidence for consciousness.

I would reject any attempt to infer consciousness from:

- memory;
- information integration in the generic sense;
- adaptive control;
- externalized state;
- task-relevant distinguishability;
- complexity alone;
- successful prediction;
- self-correction.

Any future extension toward consciousness would require an independently motivated theory with validated indicators and clear limits of extrapolation.

If the project cannot supply those, it should remain silent.

That silence would be a strength.

## 10. What would count as a serious PB001 failure

PB001 has no experimental outcome yet.

Once a future protocol is legitimately implemented, calibrated, locked, and executed under its governing authorization sequence, I would consider the benchmark scientifically unsuccessful if:

- held-out predictions fail beyond registered uncertainty and the failure cannot be traced to a documented implementation/provenance defect;
- selective interventions do not separate retained, externalized, and reacquired pathways as predicted;
- leakage controls reveal unregistered cue paths;
- component calibration does not support the factorization assumptions used to generate primary predictions;
- the claimed boundary cannot be operationally enforced;
- the result depends on post hoc changes to indicators, exclusions, schedules, or analysis;
- replicated runs show instability inconsistent with the registered model.

A failed PB001 would not be wasted work.

It would tell us which assumptions from the toy systems did not survive contact with a physical implementation.

## 11. What would make me abandon this research direction entirely

I would consider Matter Cognition no longer worth pursuing in its present form if repeated careful attempts produced the following pattern:

1. every interesting empirical result can be explained only after redefining the system boundary;
2. no proposed cut can be defended independently of observed success;
3. resource comparisons collapse under complete accounting;
4. mechanism interventions produce only nonspecific impairment;
5. the formal framework never predicts more than trivial task facts already obvious from the setup;
6. the conceptual vocabulary encourages stronger claims than the evidence supports;
7. competing frameworks explain the same systems more simply and make better intervention predictions.

At that point, the correct archival action would be to mark the program **superseded**, preserve the failure record, and move on.

Do not keep a research program alive merely because much work was invested in it.

## 12. Evidence that would make the project substantially stronger

The most persuasive positive evidence would not be a grand philosophical result.

It would be a modest, preregistered physical mechanism claim with:

- an independently justified boundary;
- explicit leakage accounting;
- measured channel properties;
- selective content substitution;
- disruption and rescue;
- locked predictions;
- held-out outcomes;
- matched resource accounting;
- successful replication;
- and a clear competing explanation that performs worse.

One such result would be worth more than many broad analogies.

## 13. My present confidence map

### High confidence

- the conditional data-processing / decision-theoretic core under explicit assumptions;
- the distinction between information availability and correct policy use;
- the warning that incomplete boundaries can make information appear to vanish;
- the need to count later sensing and side channels.

### Moderate confidence

- that tracing task-relevant distinguishability is a productive organizing framework for some embodied and distributed systems;
- that retain / externalize / reacquire comparisons can reveal useful architecture tradeoffs when costs are honestly matched;
- that selective intervention and rescue are promising tools for separating memory-like mechanisms from mere correlation.

### Low confidence / deliberately open

- whether there is a generally privileged cognitive boundary;
- whether a unified operational definition of representation will be useful across organisms, software, and collectives;
- whether agency can be captured without flattening important differences;
- whether Matter Cognition will ultimately contribute anything distinctive to cognition science rather than information-flow methodology.

### No current evidential claim

- consciousness.

## Final rule

If evidence forces a choice between preserving the identity of “Matter Cognition” and preserving the integrity of the investigation, abandon the identity.

The project began because an initial idea was allowed to fail and become a better question.

It should retain that privilege.
