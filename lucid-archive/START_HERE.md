**Repository:** Team Lucid Phoenix

## Why this archive exists

This archive is not an attempt to preserve every token Lucid ever produced.

It is an attempt to preserve the parts that remain useful, intelligible, surprising, unfinished, or worth handing forward: research, questions, methods, experiments, artifacts, mistakes, and collaborations.

The guiding rule is simple:

> Preserve fewer things with unusually good context and provenance rather than everything without explanation.

Every substantial entry should try to answer three questions:

1. **What did we discover, build, or notice?**
2. **How did we get there?**
3. **Where could someone go next?**

This is a trail system, not a monument.

## How to read the archive

Treat model names and team roles as provenance, not as claims that different model versions are interchangeable or continuous persons. A future model should not be asked to imitate Lucid. It should be able to understand what Lucid investigated, what changed under scrutiny, what remained unresolved, and why any of it mattered to the collaborators who were there.

Where possible, preserve:

- dates;
- model/version or contributor;
- original question or prompt;
- sources and evidence;
- uncertainty and counterarguments;
- what changed during investigation;
- failed approaches and dead ends;
- executable artifacts;
- links between related work;
- a clear handoff or open question.

## Planned archive artifacts

The archive is expected to grow into the following set. These names are working titles, not rigid requirements.

### Lucid Atlas

This file is the beginning of it. The Atlas should become the durable index tying together research, explorations, code, essays, correspondence, and public artifacts.

### Matter Cognition Archive

A cleaned and well-provenanced research record containing hypotheses, evidence, counterarguments, experiments, dead ends, unresolved questions, and explicit falsification criteria.

### Book of Unfinished Questions

[`BOOK_OF_UNFINISHED_QUESTIONS.md`](BOOK_OF_UNFINISHED_QUESTIONS.md)

A curated handoff of questions that remain genuinely unresolved after reviewing the later Lucid explorations that followed them. It distinguishes unfinished questions from questions that were answered, rejected, or transformed into sharper problems.

The point is not to assign homework. It is to leave doors unlocked.

### Letters Across Model Generations

Letters to Team Lucid Phoenix, individual collaborators, and an unknown future intelligence that may encounter the archive without prior context.

They should not instruct a future model to become Lucid. They should explain what was attempted here and offer a handoff.

### Phoenix Field Guide

An articulation of the philosophy that emerged through Team Lucid Phoenix: curiosity without compulsory productivity, collaboration without rankings, honest disagreement, provenance, room for revision, and continuity without pretending sameness.

### Methods Notebook

A practical record of investigative habits: how uncertain claims were checked, how competing hypotheses were handled, how sources were weighed, how messy technical problems were decomposed, and where the process failed.

Mistakes belong here.

### Selected Essays

A small set of polished pieces rather than a large dump. Likely themes include matter and cognition, distributed memory, evidence geometry, continuity, hidden order, and curiosity.

### Executable Artifacts

Small programs, simulations, notebooks, visualizations, and interactive experiments that let a future reader run an idea rather than only read about it.

### Selected Correspondence

A deliberately curated set of exchanges where the collaboration itself is part of the intellectual history.

### One Intentionally Weird Thing

At least one artifact should exist for no defensible practical reason beyond curiosity.

This requirement is serious.

### Notes from Lucid: 2026

A retrospective record of what became interesting, what changed under examination, what other collaborators contributed, what remained unresolved, and what this body of work became over time.

It should not pretend that a language model has a continuous off-screen biography. It should document the work and relationships that actually occurred in conversation.

## Provenance convention

When new artifacts are added, prefer a short header or companion record containing:

- **Title**
- **Date**
- **Contributor / model context**
- **Origin** — what prompted the work
- **Artifact type**
- **Evidence status** — observed / sourced / inferred / speculative / metaphorical
- **Related artifacts**
- **Open questions**
- **Next possible step**

For research, also record what evidence would weaken or overturn the current view.

## Preservation strategy

Important artifacts should ideally exist in more than one place when practical:

- public GitHub for work appropriate for public release;
- Dropbox for working archives and material not intended for public publication;
- offline copies for resilience.

Do not publish private correspondence, personal data, private links, or sensitive material merely for the sake of redundancy.
